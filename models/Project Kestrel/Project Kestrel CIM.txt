1. Executive Summary
1.1 Company Overview
Kalvorn Thermal Products, LLC ("Kalvorn" or the "Company") is the leading North American designer and
manufacturer of gas-fired heating equipment for livestock confinement buildings and one of the four largest
suppliers of temporary heat to the construction and equipment-rental markets. Founded in 1987 and owned
by the Marlbeck family, the Company has spent nearly four decades building a reputation for durable,
fuel-efficient and safe heaters that are specified by name by the largest swine and poultry integrators in North
America. Kalvorn designs, fabricates, assembles, test-fires and supports every unit it sells from a 165,000
square foot manufacturing plant and a 60,000 square foot distribution centre, both located in State A in the
Midwestern United States, and employs approximately 210 people. The Company generated net sales of
$42.6 million and Adjusted EBITDA of $7.9 million (18.5% margin) in the fiscal year ended September 30,
2025 and expects net sales of $46.0 million and Adjusted EBITDA of $8.7 million in fiscal 2026.
Management's plan, described in Section 1.3, grows net sales to $58.8 million and Adjusted EBITDA to
$11.8 million by fiscal 2030 without acquisitions.

KalvornAir - swine and poultry market; BrooderMax - poultry market; KA-CX - construction market
Kalvorn's products sit at the centre of the modern protein supply chain. A newly weaned pig or a day-old
broiler chick cannot regulate its own body temperature; for the first two to three weeks of life the animal
depends entirely on the barn environment, and every degree of deviation from the recommended set point
translates directly into slower weight gain, higher feed conversion ratios, greater mortality and lower grower
income. A 20,000-bird broiler house heated with under-sized or poorly controlled equipment can lose two to
four percentage points of final flock weight, an amount that exceeds the annual cost of the heating system
many times over. Growers and the integrators that contract with them therefore treat heating equipment as a
productivity investment rather than a commodity, and they buy from the supplier whose equipment they trust
to run reliably through a Midwestern January. That supplier is Kalvorn more often than any other
manufacturer.
Benefits of properly heated confinement buildings
Higher final weights and yields | Faster growth rates and shorter cycles | Sound structural and skeletal development |
Consistent feed and water intake | Lower mortality and improved animal health | Higher grower profitability and
integrator returns
International sales represented 13.3% of fiscal 2025 net sales and are the fastest-growing part of the business.
Global consumption of pork and poultry has grown steadily for two decades, driven by population growth,
rising incomes in developing economies and a sustained dietary shift toward white meat. Inter-governmental
agricultural statistics indicate that global poultry production has compounded at approximately 2.9% per year
and global pork production at approximately 1.4% per year over the past twenty years, and that the
developing economies of Asia account for the large majority of the incremental volume.

The single largest opportunity is Country A, a large Asian economy that produces roughly two-fifths of the
world's pork and is in the midst of a government-directed transition from small backyard farms to large,
modern confinement operations. Country A's environmental authorities have also mandated the replacement
of solid-fuel barn heating with certified gas-fired equipment in its principal livestock provinces, creating a
market for precisely the product Kalvorn has manufactured for thirty years. Kalvorn entered Country A in
2016 through a single distributor, appointed a dedicated Country A sales manager in 2019, supported by two
in-country staff engaged through a local service company, opened a third-party bonded warehouse in 2022
and registered a local-language brand in 2023. Two of the country's ten largest integrators now specify
Kalvorn equipment for new swine and broiler construction. Country A net sales grew from $1.6 million in
fiscal 2022 to $3.0 million in fiscal 2025 and are planned to reach $7.0 million by fiscal 2030.
Developed-market demand has its own drivers. Animal-welfare regulation and retailer commitments in North
America and Europe are lowering stocking densities and increasing the floor area per animal, and organic and
antibiotic-free programmes require warmer, more uniform environments to compensate for the loss of
prophylactic medication. Each of these changes increases the number of heaters per building and favours
radiant equipment that concentrates heat at animal level, a category Kalvorn pioneered in the poultry market
with the BrooderMax line.
In the construction market, Kalvorn is the fourth-largest supplier of temporary heating equipment in North
America with an estimated 4% share of a market management sizes at approximately $181.0 million. The
Company serves the market through the two largest national equipment-rental chains, more than 400
independent rental yards and industrial distributors, and a network of 65 manufacturers' representatives.
Construction and rental net sales were $7.2 million in fiscal 2025, a 6.3% compound annual growth rate since
fiscal 2022, and management expects the new KA-CX indirect-fired product line to accelerate that growth.
Kalvorn also sells tent heaters to the hospitality and event-rental market and greenhouse heaters to
horticultural growers through the same representative network.

The business is seasonal. Growers, contractors and rental fleets buy ahead of the heating season, so
approximately 60% of equipment net sales are recorded between October and March, the first two fiscal
quarters, and the third fiscal quarter (April to June) is the smallest. Kalvorn manages the seasonality with a
pre-season dealer programme that pulls orders into the summer, builds the high-volume lines to a stock plan
from June, and finances the seasonal inventory and receivable build with a revolving line that is largely
repaid by the end of December. Service parts, which are less seasonal, and the Southern Hemisphere and
tropical markets, whose seasons are offset from North America's, moderate the pattern. Net working capital
was 12.0% of net sales at the September 30, 2025 year-end and peaks roughly $1.0 to $1.5 million higher in
October and November before pre-season receivables are collected in December; the seasonality is discussed
further in Section 5.7 and in the quarterly detail in the Appendix.
Service parts are the Company's most profitable and most predictable revenue stream. Kalvorn has shipped
an estimated 640,000 heaters since 1987, of which management estimates roughly 410,000 remain in service.
Confinement heaters operate in a corrosive, dust-laden environment and are rebuilt every three to four years
and replaced every eight to twelve years in swine barns and every ten to fourteen years in poultry houses;
construction heaters are rebuilt annually by rental fleets. Genuine Kalvorn service parts generated $5.1
million of net sales in fiscal 2025 (12.0% of the total) at a gross margin above 50%, and have grown every
year for the past eleven years. Management estimates that replacement and rebuild demand now represents
more than half of the North American agricultural heating equipment market, which insulates the Company
from the cyclicality of new barn construction.

The Company goes to market through a combination of direct employees and channel partners. Eleven sales
professionals, organised by geography and end market, manage relationships with approximately 310
agricultural dealers and confinement-building contractors, 22 poultry and swine integrators, the national
rental chains and the representative network. The average tenure of the sales team is fourteen years, and the
four regional agricultural managers have collectively attended more than 900 barn commissionings.
International coverage is provided by a United States-based Country A sales manager with two in-country
staff engaged through a local service company and by a separate manager responsible for Europe, Eurasia,
Southeast Asia and Australasia who works through fourteen distributors.
Kalvorn's plant has received approximately $7.1 million of capital investment since fiscal 2020, including a
fibre laser with automated sheet loading, two additional press brakes, a CNC punch, a roll former and an
enterprise resource planning system that went live in fiscal 2024. The investments brought forming,
machining, welding and powder coating fully in-house, reduced lead times on the core forced-air lines to
under five days and left the plant operating at approximately 64% of demonstrated capacity on a two-shift
basis, leaving ample headroom for the growth plan without further building expansion.
The Company is led by Thomas Marlbeck, Chief Executive Officer, who joined his father's business in 1987
and has led it since 1998, and by Gregory Marlbeck, President and Chief Operating Officer, who has run
day-to-day operations since 2011. They are supported by a professional management team of six executives
with an average of thirteen years at Kalvorn and prior experience at larger industrial manufacturers. Thomas
Marlbeck intends to retire within two to three years of a transaction; Gregory Marlbeck and the remaining
executives intend to continue in their roles and to invest alongside a new owner. The management team is
described in Section 4.1.
Kalvorn's financial profile combines growth, margin and cash generation. Net sales have compounded at
6.5% per year since fiscal 2022, gross margin has been maintained in a narrow band around 34-35% through
a period of volatile steel prices, Adjusted EBITDA margin has expanded from 17.8% to 18.5%, and capital
expenditures have averaged 2.8% of net sales. More than 90% of Adjusted EBITDA has converted to cash
after maintenance capital expenditures in each of the last four years. The growth plan rests on six organic
levers - Country A penetration, developing Australasian and Southeast Asian markets, share gain in North
American poultry, penetration of the construction market with the new indirect-fired line, growth of service
parts with the installed base, and defence of the dominant swine position - and management believes
complementary add-on acquisitions, which are not included in the projections, could add materially to the
plan.

Highlight 4 - Well-established global distribution network
Kalvorn sells through approximately 310 agricultural dealers and confinement-building contractors across
North America, 22 integrator accounts that specify equipment for their contract growers, the two largest
national equipment-rental chains, more than 400 independent rental yards and industrial distributors, 65
manufacturers' representatives covering construction and hospitality, and fourteen international distributors
across Europe, Eurasia, Southeast Asia and Australasia. Many dealer relationships date back to the 1990s,
and the Company's top ten customers have an average relationship length of more than fifteen years. This
network gives Kalvorn a route to market in every significant protein-producing region and a channel through
which new products reach growers within a single selling season.
The network is also difficult to replicate. Agricultural dealers typically carry one heating brand, because
stocking parts and training technicians for a second is uneconomic, and they change brands rarely; Kalvorn
has lost fewer than five authorised dealers to competitors in the last ten years while adding more than forty.
Integrator specifications, once won, tend to persist for the life of a house design, and rental chains standardise
their fleets to simplify maintenance. The result is a distribution position that a new entrant would need many
years and a superior product to displace, and that a strategic acquirer with complementary products could use
to reach several thousand growers and contractors within a single selling season.
Highlight 5 - Experienced sales and marketing organisation
The eleven-person sales organisation is led by Lena Marchetti, Vice President of Sales and Marketing, and is
structured around the way each market buys: four regional sales managers for North American agriculture, a
national construction sales manager with a dedicated construction and tent regional manager who supervises
the representative network, a United States-based Country A sales manager with two in-country staff and a
manager for Europe, Eurasia and Australasia. The team's average tenure is fourteen years. Sales staff are
expected to be technical resources rather than order takers: each regional manager runs dealer and contractor
training clinics every autumn, supervises the layout and commissioning of large projects and is the first call
when a grower has a problem. A four-person marketing team supports the salesforce with dealer
programmes, trade-show presence at approximately twenty events per year, a bilingual technical website and
an instructional video library.
Highlight 6 - Market-leading design and engineering capabilities
Priya Natarajan, Vice President of Engineering, leads a fourteen-person department of product engineers,
research and development technicians, technical support specialists and CAD designers. The department
owns the full product lifecycle from concept through certification, production release and field support, and it
operates an in-house combustion laboratory equipped with flue-gas analysers, a thermal-imaging camera, a
gas chromatograph and computational fluid dynamics software. Kalvorn has introduced ten new or
substantially redesigned products since fiscal 2019 and holds two patents covering the BrooderMax
high-pressure radiant burner and the KalvornRadiant mid-output emitter. The engineering group also reviews
every large project layout and supports the salesforce on site, an integration of engineering and selling that
competitors do not offer.

Highlight 8 - Service-focused culture
Kalvorn supports the customer through the entire sales cycle and the product's life. Before the sale, regional

Highlight 9 - Flexible, vertically integrated manufacturing
The Company performs sheet-metal fabrication, machining, welding, powder coating and final assembly
in-house, sources only commodity components and one liquid-fuel portable heater line from outside
suppliers, and test-fires 100% of finished units. Five assembly lines - three dedicated to the high-volume
forced-air, brooder and radiant-tube families and two flexible lines for construction, tent and specialty
products - can be re-balanced within a shift, and the hourly workforce is cross-trained across at least two
lines. Capital investment of approximately $7.1 million since fiscal 2020 has left the plant at 64% utilisation
on two shifts, so the growth plan can be delivered without further building expansion. Vertical integration
also protects margin: material represents roughly two-thirds of cost of goods sold, and the Company's
engineers can redesign a part for manufacturability within weeks when steel or component prices move.

Case study: Integrator A (demonstrated highlights: 2, 5 and 8)
Situation - Integrator A, one of the five largest broiler producers in the United States, announced a multi-year build-out
of approximately 480 new broiler houses in two southern states in fiscal 2024 and invited three heating suppliers to

2. Industry
2.1 Industry Overview
Kalvorn competes in the market for gas-fired space heating equipment used in two broad applications: the
heating of livestock confinement buildings, principally swine barns and broiler houses, and the temporary
heating of construction sites, event tents and greenhouses. Management estimates the accessible market for
new agricultural heating equipment and service parts in the regions the Company serves at approximately
$252.1 million per year, of which $72.6 million is in North America, and the North American temporary
construction heating market at approximately $181.0 million. The industry uses a small number of heater
concepts, defined in the table below, that differ in how heat is delivered and in whether combustion products
enter the heated space.
Term
Definition
Forced-air heater
A gas burner heats air that a blower distributes through the building. The workhorse of swine barns and
construction sites; low cost per Btu, rapid warm-up, uniform air temperature.
Radiant brooder
A gas burner heats a ceramic or steel emitter that radiates infrared energy downward. Warms the floor and
the animals directly rather than the air, which suits day-old chicks and allows lower air temperatures and
lower fuel use.
Radiant tube heater
A burner fires into a long steel tube with a reflector above it; the tube radiates along its length. Used in
poultry houses, swine finishing barns and workshops where a wide, even radiant pattern is required.
Convection heater
A gas burner heats a chamber and warm air rises naturally without a blower. Simple and quiet; used in
nurseries, greenhouses and small buildings.
Direct-fired
Combustion products are released into the heated space. Nearly 100% fuel efficient and inexpensive, but
requires ventilation and is not permitted where people work in enclosed spaces or where moisture and
carbon dioxide are undesirable.
Indirect-fired
Combustion takes place in a sealed heat exchanger and flue gases are vented outside; only clean, dry
heated air enters the space. Required for occupied interiors, healthcare and food-plant construction and
moisture-sensitive applications.
Exhibit 20: Heater concepts
Heating is fundamental to modern livestock production because young animals cannot maintain body
temperature. A weaned pig at 12 pounds requires an environment of approximately 85 degrees Fahrenheit
and a day-old chick approximately 90 degrees at floor level; the required temperature then falls in a
scheduled curve as the animal grows. Below the recommended set point the animal diverts feed energy to
staying warm rather than to growth, drinks less, huddles and becomes susceptible to respiratory and enteric
disease. Above it the animal eats less. Because feed represents 60-70% of the cost of producing a pig or a
broiler, the return on a heating system that holds temperature within a degree or two of the set point is
measured in weeks rather than years.

Ethical and organic production trends increase heater demand. Retailer and regulatory welfare standards in
North America and Europe are reducing stocking densities - a broiler house that formerly held 7.5 pounds of
live weight per square foot is increasingly built to hold 6.0 - which means more floor area, more houses and
more heaters per pound of meat produced. Antibiotic-free and organic programmes, which represent an
estimated 48% of North American broiler production, rely on precise brooding to prevent the disease
challenges that medication formerly controlled, and they favour radiant equipment that keeps the litter dry.
Kalvorn's radiant brooder and tube heater lines are direct beneficiaries of both trends.
Construction heating serves a different need. Concrete cannot cure, drywall compound and paint cannot dry,
and trades cannot work productively below approximately 50 degrees Fahrenheit, so contractors in northern
climates rent or buy temporary heaters for roughly six months of the year. The equipment is used in harsh
environments, moved from site to site and operated by untrained personnel, so durability and simplicity of
service are the primary purchase criteria and rental chains replace their fleets every four to six years. Demand
is seasonal and correlated with construction activity and with the severity of the winter, and the rental channel
accounts for an estimated 65% of unit volume. Management sizes the North American served market at
approximately $181.0 million per year including parts.
Two smaller markets complete the picture. Event-rental companies heat wedding, festival and corporate tents
from October to April and require indirect-fired units that are quiet, odour-free and safe for occupied spaces;
the North American tent-heating market is estimated at $35 to $40 million per year and is served by a handful
of rental-equipment manufacturers. Greenhouse growers use forced-air and convection heaters with
corrosion-resistant finishes to protect crops in early spring and late autumn; the market is fragmented,
seasonal and served through horticultural-supply distributors. Kalvorn participates in both through its
representative network without dedicated sales resources, and management regards them as sources of
incremental volume for the plant rather than as strategic growth markets.

2.2 End Markets
Demand for Kalvorn's agricultural products is best proxied by pork and poultry production in the regions it
serves, and demand for its construction products by residential and non-residential construction put-in-place
in North America. Each is discussed below.
Swine and poultry
Global meat consumption is driven by population growth, rising incomes and dietary change. The world's
population grows by roughly 65 million people per year, and the middle class in developing economies is
expanding by an estimated 120 million people per year (synthetic industry statistics). As incomes rise,
households shift from grain-based diets to animal protein, and within animal protein they favour poultry and
pork, which are cheaper to produce than beef and carry fewer cultural restrictions. Global poultry
consumption has grown at approximately 2.9% per year over the past twenty years and pork at approximately
1.4%; industry forecasts call for growth of roughly 2.0% and 1.0% per year respectively over the next
decade, with nearly all of the increment in Asia, Africa and Latin America. Meat prices have risen in nominal
terms over the same period, which supports the capital budgets of producers.
Exhibit 23: Global consumption and price trends by protein, 2005-2025 (synthetic industry statistics)
Swine production
Global pork production has grown at approximately 1.4% per year since 2005 and is forecast to grow at
roughly 1.0% per year to 2035, with per-capita consumption highly correlated with income in the
middle-income economies of Asia and Eastern Europe. Production is concentrated: Country A produces
approximately 38% of the world's pork, Europe 21%, North America 14% and Latin America 9% (synthetic
industry statistics). The industry has consolidated steadily into larger, more capital-intensive operations - the
number of North American hog farms has fallen by roughly 40% in twenty years while production has risen -
and each round of consolidation replaces open-sided or naturally ventilated buildings with environmentally
controlled confinement barns that require mechanical heating.

Country A is the decisive market. Its national agricultural modernisation plan targets the migration of

Poultry production
North American pork production of approximately 15 million tonnes has grown at 1.5-2.0% per year and is

the world's cheapest meat, has the best feed conversion, faces the fewest religious restrictions and requires the

3. Business Description
3.1 History of the Company
Kalvorn was founded in 1987 by Henrik Marlbeck, a sheet-metal contractor in State A who had spent a
decade repairing the imported forced-air heaters that local hog farmers used in their nurseries and concluded
that he could build a better one. The first KalvornAir unit, a 150,000 Btu/h propane heater with a galvanised
housing and a belt-driven blower, was assembled in a rented 8,000 square foot shop and sold through three
local dealers. Within five years the Company had added a natural-gas version, a second output size and a line
of construction heaters that the same dealers sold to contractors in the off-season, and had outgrown two
buildings.
The Company moved to its current site in 1996, when it built the first 70,000 square feet of the present plant,
and in the same year it was approached by an event-rental company that wanted a heater quiet and clean
enough to use inside a wedding tent. The resulting EventHeat line opened the hospitality market and remains
a profitable niche. Radiant products followed: a radiant tube heater developed for swine finishing barns in
1999, the first BrooderMax brooder for poultry in 2004 and the patented high-pressure BM-40 emitter in
2013. International sales began with a European distributor in 2001 and expanded to Eurasia, Latin America
and Australasia through the 2000s and 2010s. A greenhouse heater line was introduced in 2008 at the request
of a horticultural distributor.
Thomas Marlbeck succeeded his father as Chief Executive Officer in 1998, and Gregory Marlbeck assumed
responsibility for operations in 2011. Since fiscal 2012 the Company has acquired and integrated a
radiant-tube manufacturer, doubled net sales, launched ten new or substantially redesigned products, entered
Country A and three Southeast Asian and Australasian markets, built the 60,000 square foot distribution
centre, implemented an enterprise resource planning system and divested two non-core sister companies - a
sheet-metal job shop in 2018 and a small agricultural-controls business in 2021 - so that the remaining
business is a pure-play heating manufacturer. Kalvorn has twice been named manufacturer of the year by its
state manufacturers' association and has been recognised for workplace safety by the state's department of
labour in each of the last six years. The key milestones in the Company's history are summarised in the table
below.

Service parts
Confinement heaters operate in a corrosive, dust-laden environment for six months of the year and require
regular maintenance: burners and igniters are cleaned or replaced each season, blower motors and control
boards every three to four years, and housings and gas trains at the eight- to twelve-year rebuild. Growers
perform most of this work themselves and buy parts through their dealer. Kalvorn estimates its installed base
at approximately 410,000 units and its genuine-parts capture rate at roughly 70%. Service parts generated
$5.1 million of net sales in fiscal 2025, 12.0% of the total, at a gross margin of approximately 52%. The
genuine-parts programme launched in fiscal 2018 has five elements:
I Dealer stocking expectations: authorised dealers agree to stock a defined list of high-turn parts for the models
in their territory.
I Education: dealers and growers are shown the warranty, liability and certification consequences of substituting
non-genuine components in a certified gas appliance.
I Promotional kits and seasonal pre-season parts programmes with extended terms, mirroring the equipment
pre-season programme.
I Pricing that removes volume discounts from dealers who are found to sell non-genuine substitutes for
Kalvorn-branded parts.
I Vendor agreements that restrict the Company's component suppliers from selling Kalvorn-specified parts to
third parties.

3.7 Operations
Kalvorn is a vertically integrated manufacturer. Rafael Tremonte, Vice President of Operations, leads a
156-person operations organisation responsible for fabrication, machining, welding, finishing, assembly,
quality, maintenance and the distribution centre. The Company fabricates every sheet-metal housing and
reflector, machines its own gas-train components, welds tubes and frames, powder coats and paints in-house,
and assembles and test-fires every unit, buying only commodity components and the sourced liquid-fuel
portable line from outside.
Manufacturing process overview
In-house sheet-metal fabrication (laser, punch, press brakes, roll former) + In-house component capabilities (machining,
welding, powder coating) + Sourced components (motors, valves, controls, steel) -> Heater assembly and 100% test-fire
-> Distribution centre
Manufacturing process
The hourly workforce of 128 in manufacturing is supplemented by 20 to 35 seasonal temporary employees
from June through November, when the pre-season orders are built. Production is scheduled on a rolling
thirteen-week horizon from the ERP system, with the forced-air, brooder and radiant-tube lines built to a
stock plan derived from the pre-season order book and the remaining products built to order. Cross-training is
a condition of employment: every assembler is qualified on at least two lines and fabrication operators are
qualified on at least two machines, which allows the plant to move labour between lines within a shift as the
mix changes.
The core lines are built to stock in market-specific kits - for example, a KA-250 configured for a Country A
integrator with a 230-volt motor and a propane gas train - so that dealer orders ship within five days and
container orders within three weeks. Configured and specialty products are built to order with lead times of
two to four weeks.
Sheet-metal fabrication occupies a 38,000 square foot bay equipped with a 6-kilowatt fibre laser with
automated sheet loading and unloading, a CNC turret punch, five press brakes, a roll former for tube heater
reflectors and a shear. The bay runs two shifts and operates at approximately 58% of capacity. Machining,
welding and finishing occupy a further 22,000 square feet with three CNC machining centres, a CNC lathe,
eight welding stations including two robotic tube-welding cells, and a five-stage wash and powder-coat line;
these areas run two shifts at approximately 62% of capacity.

The plant runs a continuous-improvement programme led by the Vice President of Operations and two lean
coordinators. Since fiscal 2020 the programme has reduced the standard hours on the KA-250 by 17%, cut
changeover time on the press brakes by more than half, halved the finished-goods footprint through kanban
replenishment of the core lines and reduced the recordable injury rate to less than a third of the industry
average. Every hourly employee participates in a weekly line meeting with a visible board of safety, quality,
delivery and cost measures, and improvement ideas are rewarded through the safety and suggestion incentive
programmes.
The distribution centre, completed in 2021 and connected to the plant by a covered dock, holds finished
heaters, service parts and export containers in racking with bar-coded locations managed by the ERP system.
It ships approximately 220 orders per day in season, with dealer orders leaving within one business day of
release and container loads for Country A and the international distributors consolidated weekly. Parts orders
are picked from a dedicated fast-moving area and are shipped the same day if received by 2:00 p.m.
Equipment
Capital investment of approximately $7.1 million since fiscal 2020 has modernised the plant. The fibre laser
and automated loader, installed in fiscal 2020, replaced two older machines and doubled cutting capacity; two
press brakes, the CNC punch and the roll former were added in fiscal 2021 and 2022; the ERP system, the
second robotic welding cell and a new powder-coat oven followed in fiscal 2023 and 2024. Management
estimates that the plant can support approximately $70 million of net sales on two shifts with the addition of
assembly labour and modest tooling, and that a third shift in fabrication would extend capacity further
without building expansion.

Every heater is test-fired before packing, with burner ignition, gas pressure, flame characteristics, electrical

failures to be root-caused within a week.

4.3 Regulation, Environmental, Insurance, Warranty, Patents and Legal Matters
Regulation
Kalvorn's products are gas appliances subject to national and state-level safety standards across North
America and to the equivalent standards in Europe and the other export markets. Each product is certified by
an independent third-party certification laboratory before sale, and the certification bodies conduct quarterly
factory inspections. The Company's in-house laboratory is accredited to conduct the relevant test protocols
under witness. Management is not aware of any pending regulatory change that would require material
redesign of the product line; forthcoming emissions and efficiency standards in Europe are expected to favour
the Company's radiant products.
Environmental
The Company's operations are subject to customary air, water and waste regulations; the powder-coat line
and wash system operate under a state air permit, and management is not aware of any environmental
non-compliance or contamination at either facility.
Insurance
Kalvorn maintains general liability coverage of $2 million per occurrence and $4 million in aggregate,
products and completed-operations coverage within those limits, a $10 million umbrella policy, property and
business-interruption coverage on the leased facilities and their contents, workers' compensation, and cargo
and credit coverage on export shipments. The insurance programme is placed by a regional broker and is
reviewed annually.
Warranty
Products carry a twelve-month parts warranty from the date of installation, extended to twenty-four months
on the heat exchanger of the indirect-fired lines, and dealers are reimbursed for warranty labour at a
published schedule. Warranty expense has averaged 0.7% of net sales over the last four fiscal years and was
0.6% in fiscal 2025; the Company accrues warranty at the time of sale based on historical claim rates by
product family.
Patents and trademarks
The Company holds two United States patents, filed in 2011 and 2020 and granted in 2013 and 2022,
covering the BrooderMax high-pressure emitter and the KalvornRadiant mid-output emitter, with
corresponding applications in other North American jurisdictions and in Europe. The KalvornAir,
BrooderMax and KalvornRadiant marks and the Country A local-language mark are registered trademarks.
Legal matters
Kalvorn is not party to any litigation other than product-liability claims of the type ordinarily encountered by
manufacturers of gas appliances, and management is not aware of any threatened claim that would be
material to the business.
Product liability - Because heaters are gas-fired and operate in buildings that contain livestock, bedding and
combustible materials, the Company receives a small number of claims each year, typically alleging that a
heater caused a barn fire. Claims are investigated by the Company's engineers and its insurer's fire
investigators; in the large majority of cases the cause is found to be a gas-supply, ventilation, maintenance or
electrical issue unrelated to the heater. Kalvorn has never been found liable at trial. Claims are settled from
time to time for nuisance value at the insurer's discretion, and the Company's self-insured retention is $50
thousand per claim. Average annual product-liability expense, including the retention and legal fees, has been
approximately $190 thousand over the last five years, and the Company accrues a product-liability reserve
that stood at $423 thousand at September 30, 2025.

6.2 Glossary
Term
Definition
Adjusted EBITDA
Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, adjusted for the addbacks described
in Section 5.5
Adjusted operating expenses
Selling, marketing, engineering and administrative expenses after the addbacks described in Section
5.5
Brooder
A radiant heater suspended over the floor of a poultry house to warm chicks during the first weeks
of life
Btu/h
British thermal units per hour; the standard measure of a heater's output
CAGR
Compound annual growth rate
Capture rate
The share of replacement-part demand from the installed base that is filled with genuine Kalvorn
parts
Confinement building
An enclosed, environmentally controlled barn or house in which swine or poultry are raised
Contract grower
A farmer who raises animals owned by an integrator in the grower's own buildings under a
production contract
Direct-fired / indirect-fired
Heaters that release combustion products into the heated space / heaters that vent them outside
through a heat exchanger
Fiscal year
The Company's financial year ending September 30; FY2025A refers to the year ended September
30, 2025
Integrator
A vertically integrated poultry or swine producer that owns the animals, feed and processing and
contracts with growers
Installed base
Management's estimate of the number of Kalvorn heaters in service
Net sales
Gross sales less dealer and pre-season discounts, representative commissions and freight allowances
Net working capital
Accounts receivable, inventories, prepaid and other current assets less accounts payable, accrued
expenses, accrued product liability and other current liabilities
Pre-season programme
Discount and extended-terms programme for dealer orders placed between April and July for
delivery before the heating season
Radiant tube heater
A gas-fired heater that radiates from a long steel tube fitted with a reflector
Regional sales manager (RSM)
A Kalvorn sales employee responsible for a geographic territory and its dealers, contractors and
integrators
Representative (rep)
An independent manufacturers' representative agency selling Kalvorn products on commission in
the construction and hospitality markets
Service parts
Genuine replacement parts and accessories sold for the maintenance and rebuild of Kalvorn heaters
Set point
The target temperature for a livestock building at a given stage of the animals' growth
