Executive Summary
I Platform
– Founded in 2009 by Dr. Samuel Achterberg, Orivane Clinical Laboratories operates a 24,000 sq. ft. centralised core laboratory in
State A, 6 patient service centres and a company-owned courier fleet serving physician practices, urgent-care clinics,
behavioural-health providers and senior-living communities across the Northeast region.
– Single-site model with an LIS/EMR-integrated workflow: 268 working days of 379 average daily accessions in 2026E, 12-24 hrs
routine turnaround and 99.6% first-pass reporting accuracy.
I Clinical excellence
– National laboratory licence and independent accreditation-body accreditation maintained without deficiency since 2016; 410
unique tests across routine chemistry and haematology, toxicology, molecular/PCR and anatomic-pathology send-outs, plus 14
custom panels built for client specialties.
– Board-certified laboratory and medical directors, a 41-person bench of technologists and net logo retention of 103% across the
trailing twelve months.
I Compelling market characteristics
– National clinical laboratory testing spend of $91.7 billion in 2025 is projected to grow to $135.7 billion by 2035, driven by
chronic-condition prevalence, an ageing population and the shift of hospital outreach volume to independent labs.
– Balanced reimbursement across government programmes (43% of net revenue) and seven in-network commercial payers, none
above 17%.
I Strong financial profile
– Net operating revenue of $15.0M and adjusted EBITDA of $3.2M (21.4% margin) in 2026E, an 8.5% revenue CAGR since 2024A
with margins expanding from 19.5% to 21.4%.
– Management projects $18.9M of revenue and $4.5M of adjusted EBITDA by 2028E, driven by the Silvermoss Senior Living
contract, two sales representatives starting in March 2027 and utilisation of existing capacity.
I Highly experienced management team
– A six-person leadership team averaging 13 years with the Company, spanning laboratory science, revenue-cycle finance,
commercial development and logistics, with a documented succession plan for the Medical Director role.

Highly Attractive Market Tailwinds
A high-growth diagnostics ecosystem fuelled by demographics, chronic disease management and preventive care
Falkmere Advisory
STRICTLY PRIVATE & CONFIDENTIAL
Project Larkspur   |   10
Executive Summary
I Adults living with at least one chronic condition are projected to grow at a 1.7% CAGR to 2035; each such
patient generates on average 3.4 laboratory orders per year, creating a recurring, non-discretionary testing
base.
I The 65+ cohort grows at 2.6% annually and consumes roughly twice the tests per capita of the working-age
population, the demographic that underpins the Company's senior-living channel.
I Consolidation wave favours scaled independent labs
– Roughly 2,750 independent laboratories operate nationally; 131 changed hands in 2024-25 as national chains
rationalised outreach networks.
– Regional operators with automation and same-day logistics win share when hospital outreach programmes are
divested or closed.
– The Company's turnaround advantage positions it as a natural acquirer of sub-scale local competitors.
I Consumer demand for novel diagnostics
– 61% of surveyed adults would take a pharmacogenomic panel if ordered by their clinician, a channel the Company
already serves through its molecular department.
I Shift to preventive and value-based care
– Risk-bearing physician groups need low-cost, fast-turnaround testing to close care gaps; two IPAs representing 640
physicians are in the Company's active pipeline.
I Hospital outsourcing of outreach laboratories
– Health systems increasingly exit outreach testing; the Company's 12-24 hrs turnaround is well inside the 24-48 hour
benchmark of national reference laboratories.

Industry Overview
Secular demand growth, technology-driven complexity and consolidation favour accredited regional laboratories
Falkmere Advisory
STRICTLY PRIVATE & CONFIDENTIAL
Project Larkspur   |   15
Industry Overview
The clinical laboratory testing market comprises routine chemistry, haematology and immunoassay testing, molecular
and genetic diagnostics, anatomic pathology and toxicology performed by hospital laboratories, national reference
chains and independent regional laboratories. National spend of $91.7 billion in 2025 is projected to reach $135.7
billion by 2035, with independent regional labs the fastest-growing setting.
I Demand expansion
– Chronic-disease monitoring and preventive screening protocols add recurring orders per patient.
– Employer and value-based programmes are widening the tested population.
I Technology and complexity
– Molecular, pharmacogenomic and mass-spectrometry assays are migrating from academic centres to regional labs.
– Complexity favours accredited operators with validated menus.
I Operational structure
– Hub-and-spoke logistics with draw sites and couriers is the winning model for physician-office volume.
– Same-day turnaround is a differentiator against national chains.
I Reimbursement and automation
– Fee-schedule pressure is offset by automation and per-test cost reduction.
– In-network status with commercial payers is increasingly decisive.
I M&A drivers
– National chains and sponsors are consolidating sub-scale independents.
– Hospitals are divesting outreach programmes to specialists.
I Digitisation and AI integration
– LIS/EMR connectivity and AI-assisted QC lower error rates and labour intensity.
– Data-rich labs can offer population-health analytics to IPAs.
Sources (synthetic): (1) Industry Research Firm A. (2) Industry Research Firm B. (3) Consulting Firm B. (4) Trade association statistics. (5) Regulator fee-schedule data. (6) Company management.
